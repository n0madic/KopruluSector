# ADR 0009: Fog of war as the original draws it, and its wider views

Status: accepted (phase 8, 2026-10-18). The user chose the original's fog drawn pixel for pixel (inline design document, 2026-10-18). Evidence: `tools/fog_check.py` against the full-game emulator's fog captures (`tools/research/sc_emulator/fogcap.py`), and the viewer's play check, which reads the view back with and without the fog and compares it with the CPU reference.

Superseded in part by [ADR 0012](0012-sdl3-runtime.md) (phase S, 2026-10-25): the fog's Defold texture uploads and the minimap chunks give way to `kp_gfx`; the fog's drawing stays the original's.

Amended 2026-11-23: the wider views are no longer an approximation. They are the original's 640 × 400 views tiled, a camera off the 8-pixel grid's too, checked against the original's own fog code (decision 4, [wider views against the original](#wider-views-against-the-original-2026-11-23)). The same day: the cells the original's partial redraws leave keep the current fog over three whole plays, so Koprulu's whole frames are the original's screen ([the original's partial redraws](#the-originals-partial-redraws-2026-11-23)).

## Context

Plan phase 8 item 5 asks for the fog of war of the original: smoothed edges, the explored-but-unseen darkening, the minimap's fog, and updates only where something changed. The original builds it in `mask.cpp` (atlas P23 `0x46b910`–`0x46c050`, P24 `0x46c1e0`–`0x46c560`) for its fixed 640×400 game view:

- `SampleAndSmoothFogGrid 0x46b9e0` turns a 24 × 17 grid of tiles, from the tile left of and above the camera's, into levels (0 unexplored, 15 explored, 31 seen) and blurs them (1 2 1 / 2 4 2 / 1 2 1, ÷ 16);
- `BuildFogLevelMap 0x46b910` expands 21 × 14 of the blurred tiles into an 88 × 60 map of one level per 8 × 8-pixel block;
- `DrawFogOverlay 0x46c3c0` darkens each block of the view through the tileset's `dark.pcx` row of its level, interpolating between the block's corner levels per pixel;
- the minimap composes its fog from the tile flags on a one-second timer (`0x46cd10`, `0x46cdc0`, `0x46cea0`).

Koprulu's view is not fixed: the adaptive screen (plan §2.1) shows any number of game pixels, and the camera may stop at any pixel.

## Decision

1. **Where it lives.** The fog is presentation state, outside the game and its saves. `native/kp_core/src/ui/kp_fog_view.c` (in `kp_ui` since [ADR 0023](0023-core-boundaries.md)) holds the original's grids and level map for one view (`KpFogView`) and builds them from the game's tile flags each frame; `kp_core.game.fog_view()` exposes it to Lua. The CPU darkening `kp_fog_view_shade` is the reference of the shader.
2. **Drawing.** `presentation/fog.lua` keeps the level map in a texture (one texel per block, the level in R; only the changed blocks are uploaded again) and the 32 `dark.pcx` rows in another. The render script's fog pass (`render/materials/fog.fp`) copies the composed index target and writes the darkened index back, after the world images and before the palette pass, as the original darkens its view after the sprites and before its interface.
3. **640 × 400.** With a 640 × 400 view and a camera on the 8-pixel grid, the grids, the level map and every pixel are the original's (checked by `tools/fog_check.py`).
4. **Wider views.** A view of W × H game pixels uses grids of ⌈(W + p)/32⌉ + 4 by ⌈(H + q)/32⌉ + 4 tiles, p and q the camera's phase on the 8-pixel grid (x mod 8, y mod 8: 0 on the grid, so the original's 24 × 17 for 640 × 400), and a level map of (columns − 2)·4 by (rows − 2)·4 blocks; the tiles outside the map are clamped to its edge as the original's sampling pointer stops at an edge. The blocks stay on the map's 8-pixel grid, so a camera between grid positions shifts the fog with the map instead of with the screen. A pixel's level depends on its map point and the tile flags alone, in the original (on its grid) as here (anywhere): the grids sample map tiles clamped by map coordinates, the blur and the 4 × 4 blocks of a tile read only its neighbours, and the darkening reads the map pixel's place in its block. A wider view is therefore the original's 640 × 400 views tiled: each of its pixels equals that map pixel in every view of the original, at a camera of the original's scroll range, that shows it. A camera between grid positions is no camera of the original, but each pixel of its view is still that map pixel of the original's views. The level map writes (columns − 3)·4 blocks: the camera's offset in its tile (at most 24 + p), the view and the next block the darkening interpolates towards; the phase p is what a camera off the grid needs beyond the original's grids.
5. **Minimap.** `kp_fog_minimap` composes the minimap's fog by the original's rules (the three map-size cases, the unexplored colour and `dark.pcx` row 15), checked against the original's composed minimap image.

## Consequences

- The fog is exact for the original's view and for wider and narrower ones, on the 8-pixel grid or off it: the original's views tiled (below). A camera's move onto or off the grid can change the grids' size by a tile, which rebuilds the level map and its texture (as a resized window does). A view wider than the map, or taller than the map and the original's 24 rows below it (a desktop window over 2,048 logical pixels wide, or a view over 2,072 high, on a map 64 tiles wide or high), has no view of the original over its pixels past the map's right (or bottom) edge: there the fog repeats the edge column's (or row's) through the clamp, Koprulu's own rule.
- A frame costs one copy and one full-screen pass of the index target for the fog, plus the level-map update in C.
- Koprulu shades the whole view every frame; the original shades only the dirty cells of a partial redraw, and the cells it leaves keep the fog of the current levels (below), so the two screens are the same.
- The fog is presentation: saves do not hold it, and it is rebuilt from the game's tiles on the next frame after a load.
- The original refreshes the minimap's fog once a second; Koprulu composes it when the presentation asks for it (the timer is the UI's, phase 9).

## Wider views against the original (2026-11-23)

The original draws only its 640 × 400 view, so decision 4 had no oracle and was marked an approximation. Its premise, that a pixel's fog depends on its map point alone, is a property of the original's code that can be run: `tools/research/probe_native_fog_117.py` maps StarCraft.exe into a bare Unicorn and runs the frame's fog chain (ResetFogGrids `0x46c050`, BuildFogLevelMap `0x46b910`, DrawFogOverlay `0x46c3c0` forced) on given tile flags, vision and camera. It redraws all 14 forced fog captures of the full-game emulator pixel for pixel.

`tools/fog_check.py --wide` then darkens views of 640 × 400, 502 × 880, 854 × 400, 1001 × 517, 1024 × 496, 1280 × 640 and 1920 × 1000 at the corners of their scroll range, at a capture's camera (two random ones, mostly off the grid, on a generated map) and at two cameras off the grid (29 and 27 pixels into a tile mid-range, and 3 and 5 pixels short of the range's far corner, where the grids' extra tile meets the map's edge), over the tile flags of every fog capture and of generated maps (all of the campaigns' sides and shapes, allied vision masks, replays), and every 640 × 400 view of the original over a part of a view must equal it. On 2026-11-23: 157 maps (22 emulator captures, 119 campaign plays' captures, 16 generated), 1,099 pairs of a map and a view size at up to 8 cameras each (at least two off the grid), 32,610 comparisons with 25,186 distinct views of the original, all equal; the original's views therefore also agree with one another where they overlap. `tools/ci.sh` runs it with `KOPRULU_CAPTURES` (the captures and 16 generated maps) and, in the full mode, over `KOPRULU_PLAYS`. `test_fog.wide_view_is_the_original_views_tiled` checks the same tiling against Koprulu's own 640 × 400 view where the original is not at hand. The GPU's fog equals the CPU's at the scripts' 1280 × 720 window (`check fog`).

A camera off the 8-pixel grid, which the original never has (SetScreenPosition `0x40b450` keeps `& ~7`): the interface keeps its own camera on the grid, but a two-finger pan (ADR 0014) moves the product's camera by whole logical pixels. Until 2026-11-23 the grids kept the on-grid size there, and the level map could end a block short: with the camera 25 to 31 pixels into its tile and the view's width (or height) a multiple of 32 or up to 6 pixels short of one (its remainder and the camera's phase past 32), the last block interpolated towards an unwritten level 0, a dark fringe up to 7 pixels wide at the view's right (or bottom) edge on an all-seen map. The grids now add the camera's phase (decision 4); `test_fog.off_grid_camera_keeps_the_fog_on_the_map` checks an all-seen map clear with the camera 1, 7, 25, 28, 30 and 31 pixels into its tile (across, down and both) and each pixel against a view on the grid, and `--wide`'s cameras off the grid find the fringe against the original's views with the old size.

## The original's partial redraws (2026-11-23)

The original redraws its view in full only when asked to (InvalidateGameLayer `0x445880`, called among others by a camera move `0x40aa90` and by ResetTileVisibility `0x46c180` every 100 steps). Otherwise DrawGameScreenLayer `0x445ea0` redraws only the dirty 16 × 16 cells: UpdateFogMaskDirty `0x46c2d0` compares the blurred grid with the one last drawn and marks dirty (`0x4cac50`) the cells of each run of changed tiles, of the tile left of the run and of the rows above and below it, and DrawFogOverlayDirty `0x46c3e1` shades the blocks of the dirty cells alone. The cells it leaves keep what an earlier redraw shaded. Koprulu shades the whole view every frame (decision 2), so its screen is the original's only if no 8 × 8 block a partial redraw leaves has other corner levels than those it was last shaded with: a block's shading depends on its pixels and its four corner levels alone.

`sc_emulator play ... --fog-redraws` (`fogcap.install_redraws`) records every fog pass of a run in `fog/redraws.cap`: the flag it ran with, the camera, the dirty cells, the level map and the tile flags. `tools/fog_check.py` follows every block of the view through them (`ShadedView`). A partial redraw must leave no block whose levels changed and must keep the camera of the last full redraw. The fog pass must change no pixel outside the dirty cells. Koprulu's level map, built from the same tile flags, must equal the original's wherever that changed. On 2026-11-23 three plays without the vision cheats ran to their victories: terran01 (`wasteland`, 64 × 64), protoss-m22 (`protoss_p1_nocheat`, 96 × 96) and zerg-m12 (`zerg_no_cheats`, 64 × 64). Together they made 106,568 redraws (1,597 full) over 50,831 frames, with at least one every frame. The levels changed in 803 partial redraws, at 140,294 blocks, all of them in the dirty cells. No partial redraw followed a camera move, no pixel outside the dirty cells changed, and Koprulu's level map was equal at all 2,028 changes. `tools/ci.sh` checks the three runs with the captures (`$KOPRULU_CAPTURES/fog/*-redraws`); `test_fog_redraws` checks the follower and the records on synthetic redraws.
