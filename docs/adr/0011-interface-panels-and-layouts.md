# ADR 0011: The interface as index-space panels in a free layout

Status: accepted (phase 9, 2026-10-24). The user chose the acceptance in three levels, the original's panel content by default with variants allowed, and an interface that adapts to any aspect ratio and resolution (inline design document, 2026-10-24). Evidence: the full-game emulator's screen captures (`tools/research/sc_emulator/uiscreen.py`).

Superseded in part by [ADR 0012](0012-sdl3-runtime.md) (phase S, 2026-10-25): the canvas in the render pass (a GUI script's own Lua context, 2 KB messages) and the Lua layout modules give way to `kp_present` and `kp_gfx`; the three acceptance levels, panels and layouts stay.

## Context

The original composes its whole screen in one 8-bit 640 × 480 surface with one palette: the game view through the console's mask, the console's dialogs (`statbtn*.bin`, `statdata.bin`, `statport.bin`, `minimap.bin`, `statres.bin`, `statfluf.bin`), the menus and the glue screens. Koprulu runs on screens of any aspect ratio and resolution (plan §2.1): a modern layout cannot be the original's 640 × 480 screen, and a whole-screen comparison with the original would hold it to that screen.

## Decision

1. **Panels.** The interface is a set of panels, each the content of one original dialog or console part (command card, status panel and portrait, minimap, resources, messages, leaderboard, the menu button, a menu or glue screen). A panel is drawn in palette indices into a surface of its own, in the coordinates of its original dialog, then placed by the layout. The indexed screen is turned into colour by the one palette pass of the render script (D10), after the game view and its fog; a glue screen swaps the palette of its theme.
2. **Drawn on the CPU (user's choice, 2026-10-24).** `kp_ui` draws a panel's content into its 8-bit surface with the original's software routines (the text renderer and glyph packets, the dialog frames and controls, the icons, the wireframes, the console art), as the original draws into its screen. `kp_ui` also composes the placed panels, the console art and the fillers into one canvas of the logical screen's size (palette index and opacity per pixel); Defold uploads the canvas's changed rectangle into an RGBA texture (index in R, opacity in A) and the render script writes its opaque texels into the index target after the world and its fog, before the palette pass (`render/materials/ui_canvas.fp`). A GUI component is not used for the panels: a GUI script runs in a Lua context of its own and a message carries about 2 KB, so it cannot receive the surfaces. A movie (a portrait, a briefing room) may stay a GPU quad over its panel when decoding it on the CPU is not worth it; the panel oracle then checks it through the render's readback.
3. **Acceptance in three levels.**
   - Behaviour: the interface's rules (ADR 0010) take input in the original's dialog coordinates, so the same input gives the same commands, draws, sounds and portraits whatever the layout.
   - Panel content: a panel's surface equals, index for index, the crop of the emulator's 640 × 480 screen at that dialog's rectangle (the captures list the open dialogs and their rectangles; the cursor's rectangle is masked).
   - Composition: where the panels go, their scale and the fillers between them are free and are checked by geometric tests and the checklist, not by the original. The `classic` layout places the panels as the original does, for debugging; it is not the acceptance.
4. **Layouts as data.** The layouts (`presentation/ui/layouts.lua`: `classic`, `wide`, more as the checklist asks) cut the console into groups at columns no panel crosses, place each group (centred as the original, or pinned to an edge) with a filler that stretches a console column over a gap, and anchor the panels outside the console to a corner; `presentation/ui/layout.lua` computes them. The logical screen holds at least 640 × 480 pixels (a portrait screen is bound by its width), shared by the world and the interface; its scale comes from the window: whole when it fits twice, else fractional with the sharp-bilinear upscale of phase 0. The layout is recomputed on every resize and rotation.
5. **Variants and extensions (approximation).** A panel may have variants of the original's drawing (for example a command card for portrait screens) and the player may turn on extensions the original does not have (tooltips, a larger scale). They draw the same `kp_ui` data differently and are `approximation` (plan §8); the original's variant is the default and the one the panel oracle checks.

## Consequences

- The original's look is checked exactly where it is defined (inside a panel) and nowhere else, so the layout can follow the screen.
- The panels' pixels are the original's by construction where `kp_ui` runs the original's routines; the panel oracle checks them as surfaces, without a render. The Defold side stays thin: one canvas texture and one full-screen pass; a movie's palette is mapped into the screen's palette as the original maps it.
- A panel costs a CPU redraw of its dirty part, a copy into the canvas and an upload of the changed rectangle; the original's panels are small (the command card is 144 × 126 pixels), and the full 640 × 480 screens change only when their content does.
- Fixed 640 × 480 screens (menus, briefings, the score screen) are centred at a whole scale with the theme's background extended around them; their content is one panel.

## Later (phase 9F, 2026-10-25)

- **The game view of a layout.** The interface's game view is the logical screen less the console's 80 rows (the original's 640 × 400 above its console, kept in the oracles): a wider screen shows more of the map at the original's height (plan section 2a, the open question §12.3's policy), and the cursor's choice, the clicks, the units on screen, the scroll's limits, the minimap's view rectangle and the sounds' distance follow it (`kp_ui_set_view`).
- **Narrow screens.** A game on a portrait window may narrow its logical screen to 502 pixels (the console's right group) with the `narrow` layout: the console in two rows, the minimap's group on top of the other's art, the leaderboard under the resources, the text box above the second row, the modeless dialogs centred by their own width. A fixed screen (the glue's) keeps the original's 640 × 480 minimum on any window (`layout.minimum`, `kp_fixed_screen`), so the logical screen changes between the glue and a game.
- **Fixed screens' margins.** The margins around a fixed screen repeat its nearest edge pixel, darkened to the glue palette's nearest colour at a quarter of its brightness (`kp_ui_canvas_margins`).
